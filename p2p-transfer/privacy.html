Privacy notice

Last updated: [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]]

In short

  • Oxfer has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and sets no cookies of its own.
  • Files go from the sender's device to the recipient's device, encrypted. When no direct path works, the encrypted data passes through our relay, which cannot read it. We never store your files.
  • To connect the two devices, some parties see your IP address: Cloudflare (which delivers the website and a connection helper called STUN), our relay, and the other device in the transfer.
  • The share link is the key. Anyone who has the complete link can download the files while the sender is still sharing.
  • We keep no record of who used Oxfer or what was sent. We hold personal data only if you write to us, if a report or a block concerns you, and for as long as section 3 says.

2. How Oxfer works

  1. Your browser loads the Oxfer app from oxfer.app. The app runs on your device.
  2. When you share files, the app creates a new random key and a share link. The part of the link after # holds the address of your device and a random access code. Browsers do not send this part of a link to the website.
  3. The recipient opens the link. The two devices find each other through our relay and set up an encrypted connection.
  4. Browsers then try to open a direct connection (WebRTC). If that fails, the encrypted data keeps flowing through our relay.
  5. The sender must keep Oxfer open until the transfer ends. Nothing is stored on a server for later download.

4. What stays on your device

The browser app stores only the items below in your browser for this site. They are not sent to us and we cannot see them. You can remove all of them by clearing this site's data in your browser settings.

You choose "Delete saved copy" or clear site data. The browser may also remove it when storage runs low.

Oxfer sets no cookies and stores nothing else on your device. Earlier versions also kept two local-storage entries written by the user-interface framework, app (which held the theme) and egui_memory_ron (interface state such as scroll positions). While a transfer runs, Oxfer asks your browser to keep the screen on. Oxfer does not use your camera, microphone or location.

If you use the Oxfer desktop app, it keeps a settings file on your computer with the theme, the folder you chose for saving, the window's size and position, and user-interface state such as which sections are open. That file holds no share links and no details of your transfers. Share links created by the desktop app also contain your computer's IP addresses.

5. What we do not do

  • We do not create accounts or ask for your name, email address or phone number to use Oxfer.
  • We do not use analytics, advertising, tracking pixels, third-party scripts or third-party fonts.
  • We do not sell personal data, share it for advertising, or build profiles.
  • We make no automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects.
  • We cannot see the contents or names of the files you send.
  • We do not receive share links, unless someone sends one to us in a report.

6. Cookies

Oxfer sets no cookies. Cloudflare, which hosts the site, may set strictly necessary security cookies, such as __cf_bm for bot protection or cf_clearance after a security check, when those protections are active for the site. Oxfer does not read them. Cloudflare describes them in its cookie documentation.

The browser storage described in section 4 is not a cookie and is never sent to a server.

7. Share links and security

  • A share link works like a key. Anyone who has the complete link can download the shared files while the sender keeps sharing. Send links only through channels you trust, and stop sharing when the transfer is done.
  • Every share uses a new key. Once the sender stops sharing, the link stops working and cannot be used again.
  • Data is encrypted between the two devices. On a direct connection the browsers use DTLS. Through the relay, the data stays inside an encrypted connection between the two devices, and the relay only forwards it.
  • Our relay and Cloudflare's STUN service see IP addresses. The two devices in a transfer usually see each other's public IP address.
  • The app is loaded from oxfer.app each time you open it, so you rely on the integrity of the files served there. We protect the build and deployment process, and the source code is public.
  • The recipient's app checks that each received file matches the sender's checksum before reporting success.

8. International transfers

  • Our relay runs in the European Union, in [[RELAY_LOCATION]].
  • Cloudflare is a United States company with a global network. Your requests are usually handled by a Cloudflare data centre near you, which may be outside the European Economic Area. Cloudflare states that, for transfers to the United States, it relies on its certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, the UK Extension to it and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and that its data processing addendum also includes the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. See Cloudflare's GDPR information and privacy policy.
  • The other device. If the person you exchange files with is outside the European Economic Area, your connection data goes to their device directly. This is part of the transfer you ask for.
  • Email. Messages you send us pass through Cloudflare Email Routing, covered by the Cloudflare safeguards above, and then to our email service provider. That provider keeps them in the European Economic Area, or processes them elsewhere only under an adequacy decision of the European Commission or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. Write to privacy@oxfer.app for the provider's name and a copy of the safeguards.
  • NCMEC. When we refer child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) in the United States, we send the report, the link or endpoint ID in it and, only where needed, how to reach the reporter. No adequacy decision covers NCMEC: the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework covers only organisations certified under it, and NCMEC, a non-profit organisation, is not. We make these transfers only when necessary for important reasons of public interest (GDPR Art. 49(1)(d)): protecting children from sexual abuse and exploitation, which EU law recognises, for example in Directive 2011/93/EU. We send only what NCMEC needs to act. For more information, write to privacy@oxfer.app.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your personal data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict its processing, to receive it in a portable format, and to object to processing based on our legitimate interests. You can also complain to a supervisory authority (section 10).

In practice, we hold almost nothing about users. We have no accounts, no request logs of our own and no record of transfers, so we usually cannot find any data linked to you. If you write to us, we will tell you what, if anything, we hold, and act on your request. Where a request concerns data Cloudflare processes for us, we will pass it on and help where we can.

Write to privacy@oxfer.app. We answer within one month. If a request is complex, we may extend this by up to two more months and will tell you why.
